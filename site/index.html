Spatial Choices, Fertility Decisions, and Educational Competition:Insights from Localizing Admission Competition

This paper studies how localizing university admission competition affects fertility, private education investment, and residential sorting. I develop a quantitative spatial overlapping-generations model in which households jointly choose location, fertility, and education spending. A Regional Admission Quota System redistributes admission pressure across urban and rural regions; in equilibrium, aggregate fertility falls and private education spending rises, while regional inequality in human capital narrows substantially.
